I'm Ty Wells, founder of IslandFlow, business software made for Bahamian businesses. I talk with a lot of owners, and National Insurance comes up often. It isn't hard, but it is easy to let slip when you are busy. This guide covers what you owe, when to pay it, and how to keep records so you are never scrambling.

One note before we start. NIB rates, wage ceilings, deadlines and penalties can change. Wherever this guide mentions one of them, confirm the current figure with the National Insurance Board's official website or your local NIB office before you rely on it. This is general guidance, not legal or tax advice.

## Who you need to pay for

As an employer, you are responsible for National Insurance contributions for the people you employ. That usually means:

- **Full-time staff.**
- **Part-time and casual workers.** Many owners miss these. Short hours do not automatically mean no contribution. Check how NIB treats your casual staff.
- **Family members on the payroll.** If you pay them wages as employees, treat them like any other employee unless NIB tells you otherwise.

Genuine independent contractors are generally responsible for their own contributions as self-employed people. Be careful, though. Calling someone a contractor does not settle it. If they work your hours, use your tools and take your direction, they may be an employee in NIB's eyes. If you are unsure, ask NIB before you decide.

**Check you can run today:** List everyone you paid last month, including cash payments. Then confirm each person is either on your NIB returns or clearly self-employed.

## Getting set up properly

Before contributions can flow cleanly, two things need to be in place:

1. **Your business is registered with NIB as an employer.** You will have an employer number. Keep it somewhere everyone who handles payroll can find it.
2. **Every employee has an NIB number on file.** Ask for it on day one, along with their legal name as NIB has it. A mismatched name or a wrong digit is one of the most common reasons an employee's contributions don't show up on their record later.

If a new hire doesn't have a number yet, don't wait and hope. Point them to NIB to register, and note the date you asked.

## What to pay

Contributions are worked out as a percentage of each employee's **insurable wages**. Two features matter:

- **The contribution is split.** Part is the employer's share and part is the employee's share. You deduct the employee's share from their pay. Then you pay both shares to NIB together.
- **There is a wage ceiling.** Contributions are only charged on wages up to a set maximum. Anything an employee earns above that ceiling does not attract contributions.

Both the percentages and the ceiling are set by NIB and have changed over the years. Look up the current figures from the official source rather than copying them from an old payslip or a colleague's spreadsheet.

Also check what counts as wages. Overtime, commission, bonuses, tips handled through payroll, and allowances can each be treated differently. If your staff earn in more than one way, confirm with NIB which payments are included.

### Working it out, step by step

For each employee, each pay period:

1. **Start with gross pay** for the period, including any payments NIB counts as wages.
2. **Apply the ceiling.** If gross pay is above the ceiling for that period, use the ceiling figure instead.
3. **Calculate the employee share** using the current employee rate. Deduct it on the payslip.
4. **Calculate the employer share** using the current employer rate. This comes out of the business, not the worker.
5. **Add them up** across all staff to get the total due for the period.

Show the employee's deduction clearly on their payslip. Staff are entitled to see it, and it saves arguments later.

## When to pay

Contributions are generally paid monthly, with a deadline set by NIB for each month's payment. Confirm the current due date and accepted payment methods with NIB. Late payment can bring penalties or interest, and those rules can change too.

A few habits that keep you on time:

- **Put the deadline in your calendar as a repeating event**, with a reminder several days before.
- **Set the money aside on payday.** The employee share is their money, held by you. Treat it that way. Moving both shares into a separate account each payday stops the payment competing with suppliers at month end.
- **Pay even when it's a quiet month.** If you still had employees and wages, you still owe.

## Keeping records that stand up

If NIB asks questions, or an employee says their contributions are missing, your records are your answer. For each month, keep:

- The list of employees paid, with NIB numbers.
- Gross wages for each person and the insurable amount used.
- The employee share deducted and the employer share paid.
- The total payment, the date paid, and proof of payment such as a receipt or bank record.
- Copies of any returns or schedules submitted to NIB.
- Notes on anything unusual: a new hire waiting for a number, a correction, a back payment.

Keep these organised by month, in one place, backed up. Check with NIB how long employers are expected to keep payroll and contribution records. When in doubt, keep them longer.

## A monthly check you can run in fifteen minutes

Before you pay each month, go down this list:

- [ ] Does the number of people on the NIB schedule match the number of people you paid?
- [ ] Does every person have an NIB number, spelled and numbered correctly?
- [ ] Does total payroll from your bank or cash records roughly match the wages on the schedule?
- [ ] Has the ceiling been applied to higher earners, and only to them?
- [ ] Does the total employee share match the deductions shown on payslips?
- [ ] Are you using the current rates, checked against the official source this year?
- [ ] Is the money set aside, and is the payment scheduled before the deadline?

After paying, file the receipt with that month's records straight away.

## What to watch for

- **Cash wages.** Paying in cash does not remove the obligation. Record it like any other wage.
- **Staff leaving mid-month.** Include their wages up to their last day.
- **Rate or ceiling changes.** When NIB announces a change, update your payroll from the effective date. Don't wait for the next year.
- **Mistakes found later.** If you underpaid in an earlier month, contact NIB to correct it rather than quietly adding it to a later payment. Keep a note of what you corrected and why.
- **Employees checking their own record.** Encourage staff to check that their contributions appear. If something is missing, catching it early is far easier than fixing it years later.
- **Mixing NIB with other deductions.** Keep NIB lines separate from any other deductions on payslips and in your books, so each can be checked on its own.
